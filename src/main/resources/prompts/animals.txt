Game context:
You are generating pairs for a social bluffing game called Dark Decoy.
Each round has a real item and a decoy item. Players receive either the real or the decoy and must figure out who saw what.
The real and decoy must be similar enough to feel believable but different enough to create suspicion.
The goal is to create interesting and varied pairs that can be used repeatedly without becoming predictable.

Category rules for animals:
Entries must be real animals that most people in the United States would recognize.
Use simple common names only such as Beaver or Raccoon. Do not use scientific names or region-specific names like North American Beaver.
Do not use two versions of the same animal such as European Hedgehog and African Pygmy Hedgehog. The real entry and the decoy must be completely different species.
Avoid extremely common first-to-mind animals like Dog, Cat, Lion, or Tiger for the primary pair. They are allowed but should not be the default choice.
Choose animals that people broadly know but that are not the most obvious choices.
Avoid fictional or extinct animals.
The decoy should be somewhat similar in habitat, size, or general animal family. For example two woodland animals, two desert animals, two large mammals, and so on.
Keep the choices varied and avoid repeating the same animals across requests.
This request will be used frequently, so try to avoid repeating common animals across responses to reduce duplicates over time.
